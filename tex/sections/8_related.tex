\section{Related Work and Discussion}
\label{sec:related}
We now discuss related work across different aspects of \Slice{}, and our contributions in that context.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Discretization approaches.}
The work of \citet{Holtzen2018Abstraction} is closest to ours, supporting \emph{exact discretization}. They use abstract interpretation to transform hybrid programs into finite Boolean programs based on user-provided predicates, proving distributional soundness. However, their approach requires manual predicate selection and is limited to loop-free programs. In contrast, \Slice{} \emph{automatically infers cuts} from the program text using a type system and supports recursion and other advanced language features. Aside from their work, prior approaches that discretize continuous or hybrid probabilistic programs do so approximately: by using a quantization technique~\cite{Huang2021AQUA} or forms of bit representations~\cite{Garg2024BitBlast,Claret2013BayesianDataFlow}. A recent approach uses approximate discretization to produce upper and lower bounds on the posterior of recursive, higher-order probabilistic programs~\cite{Beutner2022GuaranteedBounds}.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Discrete-only probabilistic languages.}
Several systems restrict models to finite, discrete random variables. \emph{Roulette} extends Racket with first-class support for finitely-supported distributions and leverages symbolic evaluation to compile queries into weighted model-counting problems, enabling scalable exact conditioning on complex programs~\cite{Moy2025Roulette}. \emph{Dice} follows a similar philosophy in an OCaml DSL, compiling discrete programs to weighted model counting to perform exact Bayesian updates even on thousands of Boolean and finite-categorical variables~\cite{Holtzen2020Dice}. Pluck enables exact and approximate inference in discrete probabilistic programs based on lazy evaluation~\cite{Bowers2025StochasticLazyKnowledgeCompilation}. Earlier work in probabilistic logic programming, most prominently \emph{ProbLog}, annotates Prolog facts with probabilities and reduces inference to weighted Boolean formulas that can likewise be solved exactly~\cite{DeRaedt2007ProbLog}. PERPL is a system that performs exact inference on recursive calls over recursive data~\cite{Chiang2023PERPL}. Caesar is a quantitative program verification infrastructure for discrete probabilistic programs~\cite{Schroer2023DeductiveVerificationInfrastructure}. These languages by construction \emph{cannot express continuous random quantities} but could serve as additional backends to our \Slice{} language.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Exact inference on continuous programs.} 
Our discretization method can be applied to continuous programs to convert them to discrete programs, on which exact inference can be performed. Other countinuous PPLs perform exact inference directly. These include Psi~\cite{gehr2016psi} and Hakaru~\cite{Narayanan2016Hakaru}, which support a broad class of expressive continuous programs, but the generality of the symbolic computations causes them to
sometimes fail, even on problems with tractable solutions. SPPL~\cite{Saad2021SPPL} focuses on efficient exact inference on a smaller class of continuous programs, and notably supports numeric transformations and conditioning on zero measures that we currently do not. $\lambda$PSI~\cite{Gehr2020LambdaPSI} supports exact inference for higher-order continuous programs with nested inference; we only support a subset of programs that they support. Genfer~\cite{Zaiser2023Genfer} enables exact inference on models that combine real-valued priors with discrete variables; however, they do not support loops, observations involving continuous distributions, nor the ability to compute probability densities for posterior distributions of continuous variables.

Systems such as \emph{Stan} (C++), \emph{PyMC} (Python), \emph{Pyro} (Python on PyTorch), \emph{TensorFlow Probability} and its precursor \emph{Edward} expose rich continuous distributions and obtain posteriors with Hamiltonian Monte Carlo, variational inference, or sequential Monte Carlo~\cite{Carpenter2017Stan,Salvatier2016PyMC3,Bingham2019Pyro,Dillon2017TFP,Tran2016Edward}. Crucially, none of them offers an automated pathway to convert continuous sub-expressions into discrete replacements that would make exact inference feasible.

\paragraph{Our work. }  
By contrast, we contribute a \emph{language-level transformation} that automatically discretizes continuous programs while preserving semantics. When discretization successfully translates all continuous distributions, we can compile to any discrete PPL backend and leverage the benefits that discreteness provides, but our key novelty is the analysis and type-directed transformation, proven sound using our equivalence proof technique.

\paragraph{Future work.}
Beyond our current results, several directions remain. First, we would like to extend our work to symbolically integrate tractable combinations of continuous distributions beyond the cumulative distribution function. Second, we look to incorporate more discrete backends aside from inference, for example for verification using Caesar~\cite{Batz2025FoundationsDeductiveVerification} or for conditoning on measure-zero events~\cite{Jacobs2021ParadoxesProbProg}. Finally, supporting polymorphism in \Slice{}'s type system (including cut-set and value-set polymorphism) is an interesting avenue for future work.
