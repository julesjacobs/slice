\section{Related Work and Discussion}
\label{sec:related}
We now discuss related work across different aspects of \Slice{}, and our contributions in that context.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Discretization approaches.}
The work of \cite{Holtzen2018Abstraction} is closest to ours. They use abstract interpretation to transform hybrid programs into finite Boolean programs based on user-provided predicates, proving distributional soundness. However, their approach requires manual predicate selection and is limited to loop-free programs. In contrast, \Slice{} \emph{automatically infers cuts} from the program text using a type system and supports recursion and other advanced language features. Aside from their work, prior approaches that discretize continuous or hybrid probabilistic programs do so approximately: by using a quantization technique~\cite{Huang2021AQUA} or forms of bit representations~\cite{Garg2024BitBlast,Claret2013BayesianDataFlow}. A recent approach uses approximate discretization to produce upper and lower bounds on the posterior of a recursive, higher-order probabilistic program~\cite{Beutner2022GuaranteedBounds}.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Discrete-only probabilistic languages.}
Several systems restrict models to finite, discrete random variables. \emph{Roulette} extends Racket with first-class support for finitely-supported distributions and leverages symbolic evaluation to compile queries into weighted model-counting problems, enabling scalable exact conditioning on complex programs~\cite{Moy2025Roulette}. \emph{Dice} follows a similar philosophy in an OCaml DSL, compiling discrete programs to weighted model counting to perform exact Bayesian updates even on thousands of Boolean and finite-categorical variables~\cite{Holtzen2020Dice}. Pluck enables exact and approximate inference in discrete probabilistic programs based on lazy evaluation~\cite{Bowers2025StochasticLazyKnowledgeCompilation}. Earlier work in probabilistic logic programming, most prominently \emph{ProbLog}, annotates Prolog facts with probabilities and reduces inference to weighted Boolean formulas that can likewise be solved exactly~\cite{DeRaedt2007ProbLog}. PERPL is a system that performs exact inference on recursive calls over recursive data~\cite{Chiang2023PERPL}. Caesar is a quantitative program verification infrastructure for discrete probabilistic programs~\cite{Schroer2023DeductiveVerificationInfrastructure}. These languages by construction \emph{cannot express continuous random quantities} but would serve as interesting backends to our \Slice{} language.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Exact inference on continuous programs.} 
We applied exact discretization to the exact inference of continuous probabilistic programs. Other countinuous PPLs supporting exact inference include Psi~\cite{gehr2016psi} and Hakaru~\cite{Narayanan2016Hakaru}, which support a broad class of expressive continuous programs although inference is slow, SPPL~\cite{Saad2021SPPL} for a restricted class of continuous programs, and~\cite{Gehr2020LambdaPSI} for higher-order continuous programs with nested inference. Genfer~\cite{Zaiser2023Genfer}enables exact inference in models that combine real-valued priors with discrete variables. However, they do not support loops, observations involving continuous distributions, nor the ability to compute probability densities for posterior distributions of continuous variables.

By enforcing syntactic restrictions that guarantee every program can be translated into a finite \emph{sum-product expression}, SPPL supports models with both discrete and continuous components while still admitting \emph{symbolic} exact inference. Rather than discretizing the continuous parts, SPPL derives closed-form integrals when possible; exactness is retained for a narrow class of models. Systems such as \emph{Stan} (C++), \emph{PyMC} (Python), \emph{Pyro} (Python on PyTorch), \emph{TensorFlow Probability} and its precursor \emph{Edward} expose rich continuous distributions and obtain posteriors with Hamiltonian Monte Carlo, variational inference, or sequential Monte Carlo~\cite{Carpenter2017Stan,Salvatier2016PyMC3,Bingham2019Pyro,Dillon2017TFP,Tran2016Edward}. These frameworks deliver high accuracy for differentiable densities but can struggle with discrete latent structure or discontinuities that arise after naïve discretisation. Crucially, none of them offers an automated pathway to convert continuous sub-expressions into discrete replacements that would make exact inference feasible.

\paragraph{Our work. }  
Our contribution lies precisely at the intersection left open by the above lines of research. We propose a \emph{language-level transformation} that automatically discretizes continuous programs while preserving the semantics required for exact Bayesian reasoning in the discrete fragment. When discretization successfully translates all continuous distributions, we can compile to any discrete PPL backend and leverage the benefits that discreteness provides.

A key achievement is that when combined with Roulette as a backend, \Slice{} enables exact inference for continuous probabilistic programs with both higher-order functions and mutable state. While SPPL supports some continuous models exactly, it cannot handle higher-order functions or mutable state. While Roulette supports these features for discrete programs, it cannot handle continuous distributions. \Slice{} bridges this gap, discretizing continuous programs to leverage Roulette's advanced features.
