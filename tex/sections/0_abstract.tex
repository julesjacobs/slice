\begin{abstract}
%Continuous probabilistic programming languages offer the expressiveness required to model complex stochastic processes and uncertainty, but they are challenging to analyze and execute. 
We study \emph{exact discretization} as a semantics-preserving transformation for recursive, higher-order probabilistic programs with continuous distributions. Our target regime is programs that use continuous quantities through finitely many comparison outcomes, where exact inference can be reduced to a finite problem. Our central technical contribution is a non-local, type-directed analysis that infers where continuous values must be partitioned into finitely many observationally relevant regions, then rewrites sampling and comparison behavior over those regions. Because this construction is global and mediated by inferred type information, correctness is not a local syntactic argument: we formalize the transformation and prove soundness for boolean queries using a coupling-style logical-relational argument over operational semantics. As an application, transformed programs can be executed by discrete exact-inference backends such as \Dice{} and \Roulette{}. Empirically, the scaling benchmarks provide asymptotic evidence: transformation cost remains small while end-to-end exact inference benefits from discretization on larger instances, with supplementary constant-factor snapshots in the appendix.
\end{abstract}
