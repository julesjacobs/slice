\begin{abstract}
Continuous probabilistic programming languages offer the expressiveness required to model complex stochastic processes and uncertainty, but they are challenging to analyze and execute. We present \Slice{}, a language and compiler that automatically transforms expressive, higher-order continuous probabilistic programs into semantically equivalent discrete ones. Our novel, lightweight type system statically analyzes where continuous variables are compared to constants, determining cuts that partition continuous domains into intervals. \Slice{} replaces continuous distributions with discrete distributions over these intervals. We formalize this source-to-source program transformation and prove its soundness using the technique of probabilistic couplings. Additionally, we evaluate the usefulness of our discretization approach for exact inference: by integrating \Dice{} and \Roulette{} as backends, we show that \Slice{} achieves substantial speedups over \SPPL{}, a state-of-the-art exact inference engine for hybrid probabilistic programs, on both new and existing benchmarks.
\end{abstract}
