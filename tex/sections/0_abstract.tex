\begin{abstract}
Probabilistic programming languages (PPLs) enable statistical modeling, but exact inference with continuous variables remains challenging—discrete PPLs are fast but limited to finite models, while continuous PPLs typically rely on approximation or slower exact inference. This paper introduces \Slice{}, a language and compiler that automatically transforms continuous probabilistic programs into equivalent discrete ones. Our novel type system statically analyzes where continuous variables are compared to constants, determining cuts that partition continuous domains into intervals. \Slice{} replaces continuous distributions with discrete distributions over these intervals, enabling exact inference via existing discrete PPL engines. For full discretization of a continuous program, our approach is limited to programs comparing continuous variables against constants; however, for more general programs, partial discretization is possible and highly beneficial too. We formalize the transformation, prove soundness, and provide an open-source implementation. Evaluation shows that when full discretization is possible, combining \Slice{} with a discrete exact inference solver such as Dice yields significant speedups over symbolic inference for hybrid programs, while maintaining exactness. When only partial discretization is possible, \Slice{} improves accuracy of approximate Monte Carlo methods.
\end{abstract}
