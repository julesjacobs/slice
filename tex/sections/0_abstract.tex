\begin{abstract}
%Continuous probabilistic programming languages offer the expressiveness required to model complex stochastic processes and uncertainty, but they are challenging to analyze and execute. 
We study \emph{exact discretization} as a semantics-preserving transformation for recursive, higher-order probabilistic programs with continuous distributions. Our target regime is programs that use continuous quantities through finitely many comparison outcomes, so exact inference reduces to a finite problem. Our central technical contribution is a non-local, type-directed analysis that infers where continuous values must be partitioned into finitely many observationally relevant regions, then rewrites sampling and comparison behavior over those regions. Because this construction is global and type-mediated, correctness is not a local syntactic argument: we formalize the transformation and prove soundness for boolean queries using a coupling-style logical-relations argument over operational semantics. As an application, transformed programs can be executed by discrete exact-inference backends such as \Dice{}, \Roulette{}, and \Storm{}. Our empirical evaluation shows two complementary strengths of \Slice{}: it supports \emph{exact inference} for challenging continuous programs that appear beyond the direct reach of previous exact systems, and on benchmarks where comparison is possible, \Slice{} together with discrete backends is fast in practice and incurs only small transformation overhead.
\end{abstract}
