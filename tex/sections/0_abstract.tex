\begin{abstract}
Probabilistic programming languages (PPLs) provide an expressive framework for constructing and reasoning about statistical models. In particular, continuous PPLs can represent rich stochastic processes where such expressiveness is essential for reasoning about uncertainty in the real world, but despite their usefulness are harder to analyze and execute. This paper introduces \Slice{}, a language and compiler that automatically transforms continuous probabilistic programs into sound discrete ones. Our novel, lightweight type system statically analyzes where continuous variables are compared to constants, determining cuts that partition continuous domains into intervals. \Slice{} replaces continuous distributions with discrete distributions over these intervals. We formalize this transformation and prove its soundness using the technique of probabilistic couplings. Additionally, we evaluate the usefulness of our discretization approach for exact inference: by integrating Dice as a backend for maximum performance on continuous first-order programs, yielding speedups over SPPL, and Roulette as a backend for programs requiring higher-order functions, recursion, and mutable state, enabling an expressive higher-order continuous PPL that supports exact inference for continuous models.
\end{abstract}
