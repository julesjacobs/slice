\section{Operational Semantics and Soundness}
\label{sec:soundness}%
%
The soundness statement in this section concerns the \emph{uniform fragment} of \Slice{}: the
syntax in \Cref{fig:grammar} without sequencing, non-strict comparisons, finite
comparisons in source programs, or continuous samplers other than $\uniform$.
We write $Expr^{\mathsf U}_{\tau,\mathrm{cl}}$ for its closed, well-typed,
fully annotated expressions of type $\tau$.
This fragment still includes discrete choice, higher-order functions, unbounded
recursion, lists, and conditioning.  We write $Expr^{\mathsf U+}_{\tau,\mathrm{cl}}$ for the
smallest closed, well-typed operational carrier that additionally contains the finite comparisons and
core-language dispatch expressions generated by \Cref{fig:discretization-code};
for this source fragment, those comparisons are finite strict comparison and
finite equality, and that is closed under the small-step rules below.  This
carrier definition assumes preservation of closedness and typing by
capture-avoiding substitution, by the transformation, and by every small-step
rule.  Those metatheoretic properties are required premises, not consequences
of the grammar alone.  The
small-step tables are exhaustive for $Expr^{\mathsf U+}_{\tau,\mathrm{cl}}$; constructs in the
full implementation outside this fragment are not covered by the theorem below.
Write $Vals^{\mathsf U}_{\tau,\mathrm{cl}}$ and $Vals^{\mathsf U+}_{\tau,\mathrm{cl}}$ for the values in
$Expr^{\mathsf U}_{\tau,\mathrm{cl}}$ and $Expr^{\mathsf U+}_{\tau,\mathrm{cl}}$, respectively.
The intended correctness statement is that, for every $e:\bool$ in
$Expr^{\mathsf U}_{\bool,\mathrm{cl}}$,
%
\begin{center}
	\emph{$e: \bool$ and $\discretize{e : \bool}:\bool$ (obtained from \Cref{fig:discretization-code}) are semantically equivalent.}
\end{center}
%its discretized expression $\discretize{e : \bool} : \bool$ obtained from the rules in \Cref{fig:discretization-code}
%
% exact discretization as follows: every expression $e:\bool$ is semantically equivalent to $\discretize{e : \bool} : \bool$. 
The first step is to make formal sense of semantic equivalence. For that, we
define a monadic operational semantics in \Cref{sec:opsem}.  Discretization
decisions are driven by non-local, type-propagated information, so a local
rule-by-rule argument is insufficient.  A complete proof must relate the
executions of $e$ and $\discretize e$ through the weak compiler simulation
stated below.  That simulation remains an open proof obligation.
%
\subsection{Operational Semantics}
\label{sec:opsem}
%
The type-directedness of our discretization function $\discretize{\cdot}$ requires our soundness argument to depend on the types of the given (sub-)expressions as well. We thus start by defining a \emph{type-dependent semantic functional}, i.e., we define for every type $\tau$ a function 
%
$
	\bigsem{\cdot}\colon Expr^{\mathsf U+}_{\tau,\mathrm{cl}} \to \mathcal{D}(Vals^{\mathsf U+}_{\tau,\mathrm{cl}} + \bot)~,
$
%
mapping each \emph{fully type-annotated} expression $e:\tau$ in the operational
closure to a \emph{sub}-probability measure $\bigsem{e : \tau}$ over values of
type $\tau$ or $\bot$. The probability of $\bot$ is the probability of an
observation failure. Missing mass represents either non-termination or a runtime
parameter error. The implementation distinguishes these cases operationally
(by raising a runtime error for invalid sampling parameters), whereas this
denotational model intentionally identifies them.
With this model, semantic equivalence captures the sought-after correctness
claim: if $\bigsem{e:\bool} =
\bigsem{\discretize{e:\bool}:\bool}$, then both expressions have the same
probability of producing $\true$, $\false$, or an observation failure.

\subsubsection{Small Step Semantics}
%
The type-dependent semantic functional $\bigsem{\cdot}$ is defined via a
\emph{small-step} semantic functional $\sem{\cdot}\colon
Expr^{\mathsf U+}_{\tau,\mathrm{cl}} \to \mathcal{D}(Expr^{\mathsf U+}_{\tau,\mathrm{cl}} + \bot)$.
Defining the latter requires us to carefully design a suitable measurable space
over the set of type-annotated expressions of type $\tau$ and $\bot$. All details
on these measure-theoretic constructions are presented in
\Cref{app:measure_space_expressions}. Let us consider the key idea here.

Let $\tau=\bool$ for the sake of concreteness. Care must be taken since, e.g., sub-expressions of type $\floattype{B}{\{3.5,4.5\}}$ cannot evaluate to \emph{arbitrary} reals but are restricted to the values $\{3.5,4.5\}$. We tackle this by extracting from each type-annotated expression a suitable \emph{skeleton}, where float constants are replaced by \enquote{holes} (hence the term \enquote{skeleton}). For instance, the expression
%
\[
	e \quad = \quad (x : \floattype{\{\clt{3.5}, \clt{4.5}\}}{\top}
	\;<\;
	y : \floattype{\{\clt{3.5}, \clt{4.5}\}}{\{3.5,4.5 \}})
	: \bool
\]
%
%
gives rise to the skeleton
%
\[
	s \quad = \quad (\square : \floattype{\{\clt{3.5}, \clt{4.5}\}}{\top}
	\;<\;
	\square : \floattype{\{\clt{3.5}, \clt{4.5}\}}{\{3.5,4.5 \}})
	: \bool
\]
%
and the type annotations tell us that the possible values these holes can be filled with are in $\Reals \times \{3.5,4.5 \}$. Subject to the carrier and skeleton-decomposition assumptions above, the measurable space over closed, well-typed operational expressions of type $\bool$ (i.e., over $Expr^{\mathsf U+}_{\bool,\mathrm{cl}}$) is obtained from the measurable spaces of the skeletons that give rise to expressions in $Expr^{\mathsf U+}_{\bool,\mathrm{cl}}$.

Equipped with this family of measurable spaces, we define the small-step functional $\sem{\cdot}$ in the expected, type-preserving manner (cf.\ \Cref{tab:smallstep-1,tab:smallstep-2}). For instance, if $e_1$ is not a value, then\footnote{Put formally, $(\mu \gg\!= f)(A) = \int f(x)(A) \cdot \mu(dx)$, see \Cref{def:dist-monad} for details. $\dirac{e}$ denotes the Dirac distribution of $e$.}
%
\[
	\sem{(\letkw \; x = e_1 : \tau_1 \; \inkw \; e_2 : \tau_2) : \tau_2}
	\quad~{}={}~
	\underbrace{\sem{ e_1 : \tau_1 } \gg\!=  \lambda g. 
		\begin{cases}
			\dirac{\bot} & \text{if $g=\bot$} \\
		\dirac{(\letkw \; x = g \; \inkw \; e_2 : \tau_2) : \tau_2} & \text{otherwise}~.
		\end{cases}
	}_{\text{standard probabilistic monadic bind over suitable sub-Markov kernels}}~,
\]
%
i.e., $\sem{ e_1 : \tau_1}$ yields the sub-distribution over expressions obtained from executing $e_1$ for \emph{one step}, and the monadic bind $\gg\!=$ \enquote{folds} this distribution into the surrounding $\letkw$-statement. The intuition for the remaining statements is analogous. Crucially, $\gg\!=$ propagates the probability of encountering $\bot$ --- an observation failure --- through executions. For instance, assume that $e_1$ from above is given by $\observekw\; (\false : \bool)  : \unit$. Since $e_1$ encounters an observation failure with probability $1$, i.e., $\sem{\observekw\; (\false : \bool)  : \unit}(\{\bot\}) = 1$, the same holds for the surrounding $\letkw$-statement, i.e., also $\sem{(\letkw \; x = e_1 : \tau_1 \; \inkw \; e_2 : \tau_2) : \tau_2}(\{\bot\})=1$.
The Boolean operators use left-to-right short-circuit evaluation: $\false\logand e$ reduces to $\false$ and $\true\logor e$ reduces to $\true$ without evaluating $e$.
%
The appendix constructs the measurable expression spaces and records the
remaining small-step measurability obligation needed to make these binds
well-defined.

\subsubsection{From Small-Step to Big-Step Semantics}
\label{sec:smallsem_to_bigsem}
%
We now define $\bigsem{\cdot}$ via $\sem{\cdot}$.  Let the ordinary kernel
$S_\tau$ take one step at non-values and return $\delta_v$ at a value $v$.
The strict bind from \Cref{def:dist-monad} extends $S_\tau$ internally at
$\bot$.  We lift it to an $n$-step semantics $\bigsemstep{\cdot}{n}$ by:
%
\begin{align*}
	\bigsemstep{e : \tau}{0} =\delta_{e : \tau}  
	\qquad\text{and}\qquad
	\bigsemstep{e : \tau}{n+1} ~{}={}~ 
		\bigsemstep{e : \tau}{n} \monbind S_\tau
\end{align*}
%
Conditional on the small-step functional being a sub-Markov kernel
(\Cref{lem:small-step-kernel}), this iteration is well-defined. We then define
for every type $\tau$ the functional $\bigsem{\cdot}\colon Expr^{\mathsf U+}_{\tau,\mathrm{cl}} \to \mathcal{D}(Vals^{\mathsf U+}_{\tau,\mathrm{cl}} + \bot)$ as follows.
%
Let $T_\tau=Vals^{\mathsf U+}_{\tau,\mathrm{cl}}+\bot$ and restrict each $n$-step
measure to $T_\tau$.  Values and $\bot$ are absorbing, so these terminal
restrictions form an increasing sequence of subprobability measures.  We define
$
	\bigsem{e:\tau}(A) =
	\sup_{n\in\Nats}\bigsemstep{e:\tau}{n}(A)
$
for measurable $A\subseteq T_\tau$.  The supremum is a subprobability measure.
This argument uses monotonicity only after restriction to terminal outcomes;
the full $n$-step measures on expressions need not be monotone on arbitrary
measurable sets.

\subsection{The remaining compiler-simulation obligation}
\label{C}

The ordinary rules above define the behavior of both source and generated
expressions.  They do not by themselves prove that discretization preserves
behavior.  In particular, the generated term for a distribution with dynamic
parameters takes deterministic administrative steps---let substitution, finite
equality, Boolean conjunction, and conditional selection---for which the source
has no matching step.  A proof-only $\dc$ operation that performs the complete
dispatch in one step does not describe the term emitted by the transformation.

Let $\Longrightarrow_{\mathrm a}$ be the reflexive transitive closure of those
administrative target steps.  The missing result is a measurable weak
compiler-simulation relation $R_\tau$ between source expressions and generated
target expressions.  It must relate $e$ to $\discretize e$, allow either side to
take finitely many administrative steps while the other stutters, couple every
probabilistic step with the transition measures as exact marginals and support
in $R_\tau$, preserve terminal Boolean values, observation-failure mass, and
missing mass, and rule out infinite administrative stuttering.  The appendix
states this obligation more precisely.

\begin{conjecture}[Weak-simulation exactness]
	\label{thm:maintext:sound}
	Suppose such a weak compiler simulation exists for every transformation
	clause in the uniform fragment, and suppose a terminal-limit compatibility
	theorem shows that its coupled finite-step approximants have equal terminal
	restrictions and that this equality is preserved by the suprema defining
	$\bigsem{\cdot}$.  Then, for every
	$e:\bool\in Expr^{\mathsf U}_{\bool,\mathrm{cl}}$,
	\[
		\bigsem{e:\bool}=
		\bigsem{\discretize{e:\bool}:\bool}.
	\]
\end{conjecture}

The earlier synchronous graph $\{(e,\discretize e)\}$ does not establish the
hypothesis: it requires one source step and one target step at the same time,
and it is not closed under the administrative reductions emitted for dynamic
distribution parameters.  Establishing the weak simulation remains an open
proof obligation; therefore this paper does not presently claim unconditional
exactness for the transformation.
